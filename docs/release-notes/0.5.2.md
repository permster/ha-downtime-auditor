**Downtime Auditor 0.5.2** lists each automation once, with its missed triggers under it, and stops reporting entities that are still connecting after a restart.

## Fixed

- **One entry per automation.** An automation with several missed triggers (for example one trigger watching several entities) was listed once per trigger, on the dashboard and in Repairs. It's now **one** finding and **one** Repairs issue, with its triggers listed underneath, each with its own confidence and evidence. The finding takes the most severe trigger's severity. Reports saved by 0.5.0 and 0.5.1 are shown grouped too.
- **Entities that are still connecting after a restart are no longer reported as missed.** "Still 'unknown' after startup" findings said nothing about whether a trigger was missed, only that an integration hadn't come back yet, and they could flood the report and Repairs. Their triggers are now re-checked when the entity reports, for up to 10 minutes:
  - an unchanged value, or a trigger Home Assistant fired itself when the entity came back, is dropped;
  - a real miss is added to the report, with a Repairs issue and a follow-up push if it's severe enough. For example, a `from: "off"` trigger, which Home Assistant doesn't fire on `unknown → on`;
  - entities that never report are listed once as *couldn't check*.

  While re-checks are pending, the dashboard and the report say which entities they're waiting for.

## Changed

- **Counts are per automation, not per trigger:** the Interrupted/Missed/Fired at startup sensors, `counts` in the `downtime_auditor_report` event, and the push. If you have automations that compare these counts with a number, they'll usually see smaller values now.
- **New event `downtime_auditor_report_updated`** fires when a report changes after it was published (an entity reported late, or a rating changed). It carries `generated_at`, `added`, `pending_checks`, `unchecked`, `counts`, `highest_severity` and `needs_attention`.
